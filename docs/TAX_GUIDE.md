# Tax guide for delivery drivers

A plain-language overview of how US federal taxes work for DoorDash, Amazon Flex and similar gig work, and how Gig Log's numbers fit into your return.

> **Not tax advice.** Rules change and depend on your situation. Confirm with a tax professional or irs.gov. Figures were checked in October 2026.

## The basics

- **You're self-employed.** Delivery platforms treat you as an independent contractor, so **no tax is withheld** from your gig pay.
- **You pay tax on profit**, not on everything you're paid:

  **Profit = earnings + tips − mileage deduction − other business expenses**

- **Two taxes apply to profit:**
  - **Self-employment tax:** about **15.3%** of 92.35% of profit (Social Security and Medicare).
  - **Income tax:** at your normal bracket, together with any W-2 income.
- **You must report it** if your gig profit is **$400 or more**.

## Filing

The gig income goes on your **normal Form 1040**. You don't file a separate return. If you're married filing jointly, it goes on your joint return. Two schedules are added:

| Form | What it covers | Where Gig Log helps |
|---|---|---|
| **Schedule C** | Gig income and expenses, giving your profit | Taxes tab → *Profit (Schedule C)* |
| Schedule C, line 9 | Car and truck expenses (mileage deduction) | *Mileage deduction* |
| Schedule C, Part IV | Business miles and commuting miles | *Business miles*, *Personal / commute miles* |
| **Schedule SE** | Self-employment tax | Calculated from Schedule C profit |

### Forms from the platforms

- Platforms send a **1099-NEC** or **1099-K** when you're paid above their reporting thresholds. For 2026 that's $2,000 for 1099-NEC, and $20,000 *and* 200 transactions for 1099-K.
- **You owe tax on all gig income, even without a form.**
- Enter each platform's reported total under **Taxes → Platform payouts** and compare it with your log. If the numbers differ, ask your tax preparer which to use.

## The mileage deduction

This is usually a driver's **biggest deduction**.

### Standard mileage rate

**Business miles × IRS rate.** Gig Log uses the rate in effect on each shift's date:

| Period | Rate per mile |
|---|---|
| 2024 | $0.67 |
| 2025 | $0.70 |
| 2026, Jan 1 – Jun 30 | $0.725 |
| 2026, Jul 1 – Dec 31 | $0.76 (mid-year increase) |

Check irs.gov each year and update **Settings → IRS mileage rate**.

- The rate **covers gas, maintenance, repairs, insurance, depreciation and car payments**, so **don't** deduct those separately.
- You **can** still deduct parking, tolls, the business share of your phone, and delivery supplies. Log these in **Expenses**.
- To use the standard rate for a car, you generally must choose it **in the first year** you use that car for business.
- The alternative is the **actual expense method**: actual car costs × business-use %. Gig Log doesn't calculate this.

### Which miles count

| Driving | Business? |
|---|---|
| To a restaurant or store for a pickup | ✅ Yes |
| Delivering to the customer | ✅ Yes |
| Between orders, or moving to a busier zone while working | ✅ Yes |
| Driving back after a far delivery | ✅ Yes |
| Personal errands during a shift | ❌ No: use **Personal errand** mode |
| Home to your first pickup / last drop-off to home | ⚠️ May be **commuting**. Logged separately; let your preparer decide |

The miles in the DoorDash or Flex app usually cover only part of your business driving. **Your own log is what counts**, and GPS captures everything while you work.

### Records the IRS expects

For each business trip, write down at the time: **date, miles and business purpose**. Gig Log records all of these, plus times, odometer readings, photos and a route map. Keep records for **at least 3 years** after filing. Use **Backup**, and keep a **PDF report** each year.

## How much to set aside

**Taxes → Set aside for taxes** = profit × your set-aside % (Settings).

- **25–30%** of profit is a common rule of thumb. It covers self-employment tax (~14%) plus income tax at the 10–15% level.
- If your household income puts you in a higher bracket, raise the %.

## Paying during the year

The IRS expects tax to be paid as income is earned.

### Quarterly estimated payments (Form 1040-ES)

Generally required if you'll owe **$1,000 or more** when you file.

| Income earned | Due |
|---|---|
| Jan – Mar | Apr 15 |
| Apr – May | Jun 15 |
| Jun – Aug | Sep 15 |
| Sep – Dec | Jan 15 (next year) |

Pay for free with **IRS Direct Pay**. Card payments have a fee.

### No underpayment penalty if…

Your withholding plus estimated payments cover **either**:

- **90%** of this year's tax, **or**
- **100%** of last year's total tax (**110%** if last year's adjusted gross income was over $150,000).

### Easier option for W-2 workers: increase withholding

If you or your spouse also have a W-2 job, give the employer a **new Form W-4** and enter an extra amount per paycheck on **line 4(c)**.

- Withholding counts as paid evenly through the year, even if you start late.
- No quarterly forms to file.

**Example:** set-aside of $2,500 for the year ÷ 26 paychecks ≈ **$96 extra per paycheck**.

### If you just pay at tax time

- Paying the full amount **by April 15** avoids late-payment penalties.
- You may owe a small **underpayment penalty**, which works like interest. On $2,000 owed it's roughly $50–$90, often less if you started gig work late in the year. Tax software works it out on Form 2210.
- If you can't pay in full, **file on time anyway** and set up an IRS payment plan.

## Other breaks to ask your preparer about

- **QBI deduction:** you may be able to deduct up to **20%** of qualified business income.
- **"No tax on tips" (2025–2028):** a deduction of up to **$25,000** of qualified tips, with income limits. "Goods delivery" workers are on the eligible list. Whether it applies to self-employed drivers should be confirmed. It does **not** reduce self-employment tax. Gig Log records tips separately so you can claim it if eligible.
- **State taxes:** most states tax gig income too.

## Year-end checklist

1. Enter each platform's 1099 or annual total under **Taxes → Platform payouts**.
2. Check that the **IRS mileage rates** in Settings are correct for the year.
3. Review shifts for missing **earnings** or wrong **miles**.
4. Create the **PDF report**, and export the **CSV** if your preparer wants a spreadsheet.
5. Make a **Backup** and keep it with your tax records.
